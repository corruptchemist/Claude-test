What this actually measures

This page is sandboxed and cannot reach any outside server, so it cannot run a conventional speed test against a distant test node. Instead it repeatedly re-downloads itself from the server it was loaded from, and times the transfer.

  • Latency and jitter are accurate. They are real round trips across your Wi-Fi, your router, and out to the host.
  • Throughput opens several parallel downloads and divides total bytes by the transfer window.

Read throughput as a floor, not a ceiling. The probe payload is small (see Payload per probe), so on a fast link the transfer finishes before TCP reaches full speed — a 400 Mbps connection can read far lower. It is reliable for what this app is for: watching change over time, and finding where in your home the connection degrades. For an absolute number, use a dedicated speed test.

Sampling every second costs bandwidth of its own; Data rate shows exactly how much. Slow the interval down if you plan to leave it running.

Keep it on your iPad
  1. Open this page in Safari.
  2. Tap the Share button in the toolbar.
  3. Choose Add to Home Screen.

It gets its own icon and opens straight to the recorder. Chart history is saved on the device, so it is still there when you come back.

One limit worth knowing: iPadOS suspends web pages that are not on screen, so recording pauses when you switch apps or lock the screen, and resumes when you return. A gap in the chart marks where that happened. Nothing measures continuously in the background without a native app.

Readings stay on this device. Nothing is uploaded.
