Page says: do evens  One formula runs this entire topic — average rate of change is the slope between the two endpoints: AROC = f(b) − f(a)b − a. From a graph, read the two endpoint coordinates. From a formula, evaluate at each endpoint first.

  2. Subtract and divide. 1 − 40 − (−2) = −32

Answer−32 = −1.5

  1. Endpoints: (2, −1) and (6, −5).
  2. Compute: −5 − (−1)6 − 2 = −44 — watch the double negative on top.

Answer−1

3Graph, on [0, 2]

  1. Endpoints: (0, 1) and (2, 6).
  2. Compute: 6 − 12 − 0 = 52

Answer52 = 2.5

  1. Endpoints: (−2, −5) (the low point) and (2, 2).
  2. Compute: 2 − (−5)2 − (−2) = 74

Answer74 = 1.75

The curve dips and climbs in between, but AROC only ever looks at the two endpoints.

Practice Set 2 · #14–17

Page says: do odds  #14 and #15 give you three points and ask you to compare three secant slopes. Compute all three, then compare — the answer to “which is largest” is never the one you can eyeball reliably.

14A(2, 2), B(8, 7.8), C(11, 9.6) — which AROC is largest?

IntervalWorkAROC
A → B(7.8 − 2) ÷ (8 − 2) = 5.8/6≈ 0.97
B → C(9.6 − 7.8) ÷ (11 − 8) = 1.8/30.6
A → C(9.6 − 2) ÷ (11 − 2) = 7.6/9≈ 0.84

AnswerA to B is the largest, at about 0.97.

The curve is steepest in the middle and flattens out near C, so any interval ending at C gets dragged down. Notice A→C lands between the other two — an average over a longer span always sits between the pieces it covers.

15A(2, 5), B(4, 8), C(8, 11) — which AROC is smallest?

IntervalWorkAROC
A → B(8 − 5) ÷ (4 − 2) = 3/21.5
B → C(11 − 8) ÷ (8 − 4) = 3/40.75
A → C(11 − 5) ÷ (8 − 2) = 6/61

AnswerB to C is the smallest, at 0.75.

Both A→B and B→C climb the same 3 units, but B→C spreads that climb over twice the run, so its rate is half as big. The graph is concave down — still rising, but rising more slowly the further right you go.

17C(p) = 18 − p² + p, price from $3 to $4

  1. Demand at $3. C(3) = 18 − 9 + 3 = 12 treats.
  2. Demand at $4. C(4) = 18 − 16 + 4 = 6 treats.
  3. Average rate of change. 6 − 124 − 3 = −6

Answer−6 treats per dollar. Because the rate is negative, demand falls as price rises: each $1 increase costs an average of 6 treats of demand, and this customer buys half as many treats at $4 as at $3.

The second sentence of the question is asking for that interpretation in words — write it out, with units. “Treats per dollar” is the unit, because the output is treats and the input is dollars.

Applications · #18–20

Page says: do evens  Same formula, but now the answer needs units and a sentence. The units always come straight from the axes: output-units per input-unit.

18Beetles: P(x) = 24(2x) over the first five weeks [0, 5]

  1. Start. P(0) = 24(20) = 24(1) = 24 beetles.
  2. End. P(5) = 24(25) = 24(32) = 768 beetles.
  3. Average growth rate. 768 − 245 − 0 = 7445

Answer148.8 beetles per week

Notice this average badly under-describes the end of the period: the population grows by only 24 in week one but by 384 in week five. Exponential growth is exactly the case where a single average rate hides the story.

19Cost: C(x) = 0.0002x³ + 0.09x² + 12x + 1500

  1. A. From 100 to 300 widgets.
    C(100) = 0.0002(1{,}000{,}000) + 0.09(10{,}000) + 1200 + 1500 = 200 + 900 + 1200 + 1500 = 3800
    C(300) = 0.0002(27{,}000{,}000) + 0.09(90{,}000) + 3600 + 1500 = 5400 + 8100 + 3600 + 1500 = 18{,}600
    AROC = 18{,}600 − 3800300 − 100 = 14{,}800200 = 74
  2. B. From 200 to 500 widgets.
    C(200) = 1600 + 3600 + 2400 + 1500 = 9100
    C(500) = 25{,}000 + 22{,}500 + 6000 + 1500 = 55{,}000
    AROC = 55{,}000 − 9100500 − 200 = 45{,}900300 = 153

AnswersA. $74 per widget  ·  B. $153 per widget

This is marginal cost: the average extra cost of each additional widget over that range. It more than doubles, which says production is getting less efficient at higher volumes.

20Projectile: h(t) = −16t² + 48t + 80

  1. Heights first.
    h(0) = 80 ft  ·  h(2) = −64 + 96 + 80 = 112 ft  ·  h(4) = −256 + 192 + 80 = 16 ft
  2. A. First two seconds. 112 − 802 − 0 = 322 = 16
  3. B. From t = 2 to t = 4. 16 − 1124 − 2 = −962 = −48

AnswersA. 16 ft/sec  ·  B. −48 ft/sec

Average rate of change of height is exactly average velocity. Positive means it's higher at the end than the start (still going up on balance); negative means it's on the way down. The object peaks at t = 1.5 s, which is why the second interval is negative.

Two things the blanks are really testing. Even vs. odd is a computation, not a vibe: substitute −x and see whether you get f(x) back (even, y-axis symmetry) or −f(x) (odd, origin symmetry). One-to-one is the horizontal line test — and it's exactly the functions that never turn around (linear, cubic, square root) that pass it. The ones with a vertex or a turning point (constant, absolute value, quadratic) fail.
